# Week-08 — Resources

This is everything I used this week to complete **Course 8**, the final course of the **Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate** on Coursera — **Put It to Work: Prepare for Cybersecurity Jobs**.
I'm documenting this so anyone starting from scratch can follow the same path.
Courses 6 and 7 handed me an alert queue and a script editor. This week handed me a resume template and a mock interview — the certificate stopped teaching tools and started teaching how to actually walk into a room (or a Zoom call) and talk about them.

---

## The Resources That Shaped This Week

Course 8 is different from every course before it — there's no lab environment, no simulated SIEM, no code editor. It's entirely about translating seven courses of technical work into a resume, a portfolio, a professional network, and answers you can give under pressure in an interview.

Coursera was still the backbone, but this week I leaned much harder on outside resources — actual job postings, LinkedIn, and real portfolio examples from other analysts — because the course's advice only becomes useful once you apply it to something real.

## Course Activities — Where the Theory Got Applied

This week's activities weren't hands-on-keyboard technical labs — they were career-building deliverables meant to be reused for real applications.

### What I Completed
| Activity | Course | What It Involved |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Resume Draft | Course 8 | Rewrote my resume to highlight transferable and technical cybersecurity skills |
| Portfolio Review | Course 8 | Organized this GitHub repo as a work-in-progress portfolio piece |
| Elevator Pitch Exercise | Course 8 | Practiced a 30-second summary of my cybersecurity background and goals |
| Mock Interview Activity | Course 8 | Answered common behavioral and technical interview questions |
| Job Search Strategy Activity | Course 8 | Identified relevant entry-level titles and where to search for them |

### Why This Mattered
Writing seven weeks of notes is one thing — compressing them into a resume bullet or a 30-second answer is a completely different skill. The mock interview activity was the part that actually exposed which topics I understood well enough to explain out loud, versus which ones I only understood well enough to recognize on a quiz.

## My Recommendation to Anyone Starting This Path

Don't treat Course 8 as an afterthought. It's tempting to rush through it after the technical weight of Courses 6 and 7, but this is the course that turns seven weeks of learning into something an employer can actually see and evaluate.
Actually say your elevator pitch out loud, more than once. Actually do the mock interview instead of skimming the question list. The gap between "I understand this" and "I can explain this to someone else" is bigger than it looks until you're forced to close it.

Last week was about watching systems and writing code to watch them for you. This week was about explaining, to another human, why any of that matters. Eight courses down — this is the one that gets you from "certificate holder" to "candidate."
